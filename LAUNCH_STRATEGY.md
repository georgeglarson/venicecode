# VeniceCode Launch Strategy

## 1. Positioning

**Primary Positioning:** *The Venice.ai-Optimized Coding Agent for TypeScript Developers.*

**Secondary Positioning:** *The community-driven, privacy-focused successor to OpenCode.*

Our goal is to capture the abandoned OpenCode community and attract new users from the TypeScript ecosystem who value privacy and performance. We are not competing directly with `crush` on its home turf (the Go community); instead, we are carving out a new niche.

## 2. Target Audience

- **Primary:** Developers who were using `opencode` and are now looking for an alternative.
- **Secondary:** TypeScript/JavaScript developers who want an AI coding agent that integrates with their existing stack.
- **Tertiary:** Privacy-conscious developers who are drawn to the Venice.ai platform.

## 3. Key Messaging

- **"The OpenCode you loved, but better."** (Familiarity and improvement)
- **"Built for Venice.ai, built for privacy."** (Privacy and optimization)
- **"Your AI coding agent, now in TypeScript."** (Accessibility and community)
- **"Actively maintained, community-driven."** (Trust and collaboration)

## 4. Launch Channels

- **Hacker News:** A "Show HN" post is critical. The title should be something like: "Show HN: VeniceCode - A community fork of OpenCode, now on TypeScript and optimized for Venice.ai"
- **Reddit:** Post in `r/typescript`, `r/javascript`, `r/node`, and relevant AI/developer tool subreddits.
- **Twitter/X:** Announce the launch and tag relevant influencers and communities (Venice.ai, Bun, etc.).
- **GitHub:** Post in the `opencode-ai/opencode` issues to let users know there is an alternative.
- **Venice.ai Community:** Announce in the Venice.ai Discord/forums.
- **Dev.to / Medium:** A longer-form blog post explaining the story and vision behind VeniceCode.

## 5. Launch Phases

### Phase 1: Pre-Launch (Now)

- **Finalize README:** Ensure it is clear, compelling, and has all the necessary information.
- **Create Marketing Materials:** Prepare the Show HN post, Reddit posts, tweets, and blog post.
- **Prepare Community Outreach Templates:** Write a standard message to post in OpenCode issues.
- **Final Polish:** Ensure the codebase is clean, well-documented, and easy to install.

### Phase 2: Launch Day

- **Post Everywhere:** Simultaneously post on Hacker News, Reddit, Twitter, and Dev.to.
- **Engage:** Be prepared to answer questions, respond to comments, and engage with the community in real-time.
- **GitHub Outreach:** Post the community outreach message in the most active `opencode` issues.

### Phase 3: Post-Launch (First Week)

- **Monitor Feedback:** Triage bug reports, feature requests, and general feedback.
- **First Patch:** Release a `v1.0.1` with any quick fixes or improvements based on community feedback.
- **Thank the Community:** Post a follow-up message thanking everyone for their support and outlining the next steps.

## 6. Community Engagement

- **Discord/Slack:** Create a dedicated community space for users and contributors.
- **Public Roadmap:** Use GitHub Projects to create a public roadmap and show what you're working on.
- **Contributor Guidelines:** Make it easy for new contributors to get involved.
- **Regular Updates:** Post weekly or bi-weekly updates on your progress.

## 7. Success Metrics

- **GitHub Stars:** Aim for 500+ stars in the first month.
- **Forks & Contributors:** Aim for 10+ contributors in the first three months.
- **Community Growth:** Measure the number of members in your Discord/Slack.
- **User Feedback:** Track the number of positive comments and feature requests.
